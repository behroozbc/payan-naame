\section{LLM as Judge}

Using an LLM as a judge is an effective approach for evaluating generated answers and providing feedback on their quality.
The judge LLM is a model with a larger number of parameters, specifically 'deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash' \cite{deepseekai2026deepseekv4}.
This method raises several concerns, as LLMs are not entirely trustworthy
and do not provide explainable reasoning for their decisions.

For the answer relevance metric, an LLM was employed to compare the generated answer against the correct answer
and the prerequisites of each question, enabling the model to verify whether all required concepts were adequately covered.

The evaluation framework employed in this study leverages LLM-as-a-judge to assess three key dimensions of the RAG pipeline's performance, each capturing a distinct aspect of retrieval and generation quality.

\textbf{Contextual Relevancy} evaluates the quality of the retriever component by measuring the overall relevance of the information contained within the retrieved context with respect to a given input. This metric operates by employing the LLM judge to assess whether the retrieved passages contain information pertinent to the user's query. A higher contextual relevancy score indicates that the retriever is effectively selecting documents that are topically aligned with the input, thereby providing the generator with a solid informational foundation.

\textbf{Faithfulness} measures the quality of the generator by evaluating whether the generated output factually aligns with the contents of the retrieved context. This metric addresses a critical concern in RAG systems: the tendency of language models to hallucinate or introduce information not supported by the provided context. A high faithfulness score indicates that the generator adheres closely to the retrieved evidence, producing answers that are grounded in the source material rather than relying solely on parametric knowledge.

\textbf{Answer Relevancy} evaluates the degree to which the generated answer directly addresses the user's question. This metric examines whether the output contains information that is responsive to the query, avoiding extraneous or off-topic content. A high answer relevancy score reflects the system's ability to produce concise and targeted responses that meet the informational needs of the user.

All three metrics are implemented as self-explaining LLM-Evals, meaning that in addition to producing a numerical score, the LLM judge generates a natural language explanation justifying its assessment. This interpretability is valuable for diagnosing specific failure modes within the pipeline and for iteratively refining system components.

As illustrated in the table, the zero-shot baseline and the context-augmented baseline exhibit substantial differences across all metrics, yet they also share certain similarities. Notably, answer relevancy shows a marked improvement in the context-augmented baseline, likely because the generated answers incorporate content from the course materials.

A notable observation is that the generated answers often fail to pass the answer relevancy evaluation because the model tends to default to summarizing the input rather than directly addressing the question. Upon reviewing the model's chain-of-thought reasoning for each answer, a recurring pattern emerges: initially, the model correctly identifies the need to answer the user's question directly. However, as the chain-of-thought progresses deeper into the reasoning process---particularly when listing prerequisites---the model loses sight of the original question. For instance, when asked "What is an AI agent?", the model shifts its focus to producing a comprehensive summary of the prerequisites rather than providing a direct and concise answer to the question posed.

To further investigate this issue, an additional experiment was conducted in which the same question was posed to the fine-tuned model without providing any prior context or instructions regarding academic tone. The objective was to assess the model's ability to answer the question independently, without any guidance or preferences. Interestingly, the resulting answers exhibited a markedly improved academic tone and overall quality compared to those generated with context and prerequisite information. This suggests that, in certain cases, providing additional context may inadvertently lead the model away from producing a direct and well-formulated response, and that a standalone question without supplementary instructions can yield superior results.

However, relying solely on a fine-tuned model is not a sufficient solution for this approach, as the degree of customization required as mentioned on research questions at the beginning. Therefore, a mechanism that can be adapted at runtime is necessary, rather than relying on a fixed model from the outset.
